intro: >
  Crème brûlée is a popular dessert, consisting of a custard base with
  caramelized sugar on top. My version doesn’t require any baking so you don’t
  need to worry about overbaking and honestly doesn’t require much work. Also,
  unlike the traditional dessert, I use goguma (Korean sweet potatoes) which
  gives it a unique sweet and nutty flavor. You can use either Korean sweet
  potatoes or if you want some color, purple sweet potatoes taste good as well!

ingredients:
  - eggs
  - milk
  - heavy cream
  - sugar
  - sweet potatoes
  - vanilla extract
  - salt
  - cornstarch
instructions:
  - Steam your sweet potatoes. For this recipe (makes about 4 servings), we’ll
    be using 2. It takes about 20-30 minutes on medium-high heat, but you’ll
    know when it’s done when you’re able to poke the potatoes without any
    resistance. In this case, it’s better to over steam than undercook because
    we’re going to be incorporating the potatoes into the cream later.
  - >
    While potatoes steam, separate 2 egg yolks into a bowl and add 30g of sugar
    and a dash of vanilla extract. Mix with a whisk until combined.
  - In a pot, pour ¾ cup of cream and ¼ cup of milk and bring to a simmer.
  - "Once you have your egg yolk mixture and your heated cream/milk, we’re going
    to temper the egg yolks and make the base custard. This will be the hardest
    step but if you take your time and whisk constantly, I promise you won’t get
    scrambled eggs. Start by taking a spoonful of the cream and mixing it into
    the egg yolks. Constantly mix so the eggs don’t cook through but rather
    bring the temperature up. Repeat this process until all the cream is added
    in. "
  - While whisking, you might start to notice bubbly foam appear on top. Don’t
    worry about it too much! The main thing to check is to make sure your
    custard is silky smooth. From there, we’re going to strain the base custard
    into a blender or if you have an emulsion blender, you can add it directly
    into a pot.
  - Peel your sweet potatoes and put 1 whole sweet potato and half of the other
    one into the custard. Blend until the potatoes are incorporated well into
    the custard. It should be thick and smooth. If it feels grainy, you may need
    to blend a bit longer (or you might’ve added too much sweet potatoes but
    it’ll still taste good!)
  - Now we’re going to heat up the custard once more to thicken it even more
    into an almost pudding-like consistency. On low-medium heat and constantly
    whisking, cook the custard until it starts to look like it’s getting
    thicker. At this point, put about a half spoonful of cornstarch and mix it
    in well. Since the potatoes already have starch in them naturally, just a
    little bit of cornstarch is all that’s needed or you’ll get a custard that
    is too pasty. The final product should be able to coat the back of a
    spoon/spatula.
  - Time to cool! Pour the custard evenly into 4 ramekins and let them cool to
    room temp before putting plastic wrap and into the fridge overnight. If
    you’re short on time, you can put them into the freezer after letting them
    cool down initially. But if you go this route, only put it in the freezer
    for 10-30 minutes or the texture might be unpleasant. (Also, if you want,
    you can chop up the other half sweet potato into tiny pieces and add it in
    to give more texture and stronger goguma taste)
  - When your custard is cooled down, it should be set. Sprinkle sugar on top
    and either torch it or put it right under a broiler on high until the sugar
    melts and caramelizes. Crack the shell with a spoon and enjoy!
