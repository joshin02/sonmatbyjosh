---
title: chawanmushi
image: /uploads/chawanmushi.jpg
intro: Chawanmushi is a Japanese steamed egg custard that is super soft, very
  similar to soondoobu texture. It’s super versatile in what you can add as
  toppings. The first time I tried chawanmushi was back in 2021 when I lived in
  Japan for about a year and a half. I’m not a super big fan of eggs, but this
  doesn’t even taste like eggs??? The taste really depends on the toppings, but
  generally it has a seafood profile from the dashi. The ratio of egg to dashi
  will make or break the dish so I wouldn’t say it’s the simplest recipe but
  definitely not difficult if you follow the recipe to a tea (no pun intended).
  Once you get the ratio down, feel free to play with various toppings!
ingredients:
  - eggs
  - green onion
  - katsuobushi
  - kombu
  - soy sauce
  - salt
  - mirin
instructions:
  - >
    The first step is to make the dashi. I prefer to make my own dashi stock to
    control the flavors but if you want something a lot simpler, boiling some
    water with hon dashi packets is completely ok and it will still taste great!
    If you’re making your own dashi, put about 2 cups of water in a pot with 3-4
    pieces of kombu and bring to boil. Skim off any foam and then add about 1-2
    handfuls of katsuobushi and turn off heat. Add a dash of soy sauce and
    mirin, and then salt to taste. After 10 minutes of steeping, strain soup
    out. (make sure to squeeze any liquid from the katsuobushi, this is where a
    lot of the flavor is)
  - "After the dashi is COMPLETELY cool, this is the ratio you want to use: 3:1
    (eggs: dashi + mirin). I like to use a scale for more accuracy but cup
    measurements are fine too. In terms of dashi and mirin, you only need a
    splash of mirin so it’s like 97% dashi. The reason I combine dashi and mirin
    as a single part is because adding mirin (a liquid) to a ratio that is
    already 3:1 has yielded watery chawanmushi in my experience"
  - >
    Pour into tea cups or tiny bowls that are heat proof and either cover with
    the tea cup lids or aluminum foil
  - Put in a steamer for about 15-20 minutes. You can uncover and check if
    you’re unsure about the eggs being cooked through. It shouldn’t be watery
    and should have a bit of a jiggle when you tap it with a spoon.
  - Carefully remove chawanmushi from the steamer (be careful, the cups/bowls
    will be hot!!) Enjoy 🙂
